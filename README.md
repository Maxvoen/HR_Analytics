## Project Overview

The analysis examines attrition patterns across key workforce dimensions, including age groups, education levels, job levels, and job roles, and departments to identify the employee segments with the highest turnover, make recommendations, and gain deeper understanding into employee attrition within the organization to assess workforce stability and identify opportunities for improving employee retention

## Data Analysis

### Findings

- Education Level

The analysis indicates that employee retention differs substantially across education levels. Bachelor’s Degree (the largest workforce: 1,141, Attrition Rate: 10.52%) and Master’s Degree (Workforce: 794, Lowest Attrition Rate: 1.26%), Doctoral’ Degree (Smallest workforce: 96, highest Attrition Rate: 98.88%).
Nearly all employees with Doctoral Degrees are leaving the organization, representing a critical talent retention issue.
However, because there are only 96 employees in this category, the extremely high attrition rate may be influenced by the small sample size or specific organizational circumstances.
Bachelor's degree employees provide the largest and relatively stable workforce, making them a critical asset for sustaining business operations.

- Job Level
  
Level 1 (Workforce: 1077, Attrition Rate: 27.67%), Level 2 (Workforce: 1064, Attrition Rate: 99.6%),, Level 3, (Workforce: 434, Attrition Rate: 15.44%), Level 4 (Workforce: 212, Attrition Rate: 5.19%), Level 5 (Workforce: 138, Attrition Rate: 7.25%).
Level 1 is the primary attrition hotspot, accounting for the highest turnover among the largest employee population. This should be the organization's top retention priority.
Attrition generally declines as job level increases, suggesting that career advancement, better compensation, increased responsibility, and improved engagement contribute to stronger employee retention.

- Job Role

Sales Executive (Workforce: 651, Attrition Rate: 25.54), Research (Workforce:580, Attrition Rate: 17.07%), Laboratory Technician (Workflow: 509, 25.54%), Manufacturing Director (Workforce: 288, Attrition Rate: 7.99%), Healthcare Representative (Workforce: 261, Attrition: 7.28%), Manager (Workforce: 204, Attrition Rate: 5.39%), Sales Representative (Workforce: 168, Attrition Rate: 40.48%), Research Director ( Workforce: 160, Attrition Rate: 3.13%), Human Resources (Workforce: 104, Attrition Rate:23.08%).
Sales Representatives (40.48%) and Sales Executives (25.54%) have the highest turnover, directly threatening revenue generation and customer relationships.
High HR turnover can weaken recruitment, employee engagement, policy implementation, and overall workforce management effectiveness.

- Department

Sales Department (186 employees|20.8% Attrition Rate), Human Resources Department (24 employees|19.0% Attrition Rate), Research & Development (282 employees|14.8% Attrition Rate).
Sales has the highest attrition rate among the three departments. High turnover can result in lost customer relationships, lower revenue generation, increased recruitment costs, and disruptions in sales targets.
HR attrition can negatively affect recruitment, onboarding, employee engagement initiatives, and overall workforce planning. A small HR team experiencing turnover may struggle to effectively support organisation’s talent management strategy.
R&D has the largest workforce and comparatively lower attrition rate, suggesting stronger employee retention. However, because of its large headcount, employee departures can still lead to the loss of critical technical knowledge, project delays, and reduced innovation development.

- Age Group

Under 25 (194 employees| 11.86% Attrition Rate), Under 26 (1 employee| 100% Attrition Rate), Under 27 (1 employee| 100% Attrition Rate), 25 -34 (1098 employees| 21.22% Attrition Rate), 35 – 34 (1005 employees| 10.34% Attrition Rate), 45 – 54 (489 employees| 16.16% Attrition Rate), Over 55 (137 employees| 37.23% Attrition Rate).
The 25–35 age group is the organisation's largest talent pool and contributes the greatest volume of employee turnover, making it the highest priority for retention initiatives.
The Over 55 age group exhibits the highest meaningful attrition rate, increasing the risk of losing experienced employees, leadership capability, and institutional knowledge.
Employees aged 35–44 represent the most stable segment of the workforce and can serve as mentors and successors for younger employees.
Very small categories (Under 26 and Under 27) produce misleading 100% attrition rates due to insufficient sample sizes and should not be used for strategic conclusions.

### Recommendations

1. Conduct Department-Specific Exit Analysis
   - Analyse exit interview data to identify the primary reasons employees leave each department
   - Develop targeted retention strategies based on department-specific challenges rather than applying a single organization-wide approach
2. Improve Employee Engagement Across Departments
   - Regularly monitor departmental attrition trends using HR dashboards.
   - Recognise high-performing employees and provide clear opportunities for career growth to improve long-term retention
3. Strengthen Sales
   - Review compensation, incentives, and commission structures.
4. Improve work-life balance initiatives where workload or stress is a major concern.
5. Strengthen Career Development
   - Create structured learning and promotion pathways for employees
